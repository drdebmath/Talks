Words We'll Use

AI agent
An AI program that does tasks for you, step by step. It does not just answer.
chatbot
An AI program that only answers questions.
tool
Something an AI agent can use: a browser, a phone, a calendar, a crypto wallet.
payment system
The way money travels from one person to another: cards, UPI, banks, blockchains.
fee
What a payment system charges for one payment.
MDR
Merchant discount rate: the fee a shop pays on each digital payment, as a % of the sale.
profit margin
The share of each sale a shop keeps as profit.
bundled payment
Many tiny payments added up and paid once.
blockchain
A shared public record of payments that no single company controls.
stablecoin
A crypto coin that is always worth one US dollar, such as USDC.
crypto wallet
An app that holds crypto and approves payments.
smart wallet
A crypto wallet with spending rules built in.
spending limit
The most an AI agent may spend, per day or per payment.
L2 network
A cheaper, faster lane built on top of Ethereum.
x402
A way for an AI agent to pay a website inside the web request itself.
ERC-8004
A public ID card and rating record for AI agents on Ethereum.
locked payment
Money held by code until the work is checked.
agent economy
AI agents buying and selling, from people and from each other.
trick message
Hidden text that makes an AI agent do something its owner did not want.
ID check
How a bank confirms who you are before opening an account.
self-reported
A number the poster or company gave. Nobody else has checked it.

Can an Agent Open a Bank Account?

Identity

A bank's ID check expects a person, not software.

Hours

Banks close on weekends and holidays. Agents don't.

Size

A fixed fee makes paying ₹0.01 impossible.

Screens

Checkout pages, CAPTCHAs, OTPs — built for human eyes.

Paying ₹0.01 for One Request

Card fixed fee ($0.30)
≈ ₹26
Ethereum main network
≈ ₹1–5
L2 network · USDC
≈ ₹0.1–1
The payment itself
₹0.01

Every fee is bigger than the payment. The fix is a bundled payment: add up many tiny payments, pay once.

Rough figures at ≈ ₹88 per US dollar; blockchain fees change with how busy the network is. | stripe.com/pricing | l2fees.info

When the Fee Eats the Profit

Shop with 1% profit margin
₹1.00 profit − ₹0.40 MDR = ₹0.60
Shop with 0.3% profit margin
₹0.30 profit − ₹0.40 MDR = −₹0.10

Per ₹100 of sales, with an MDR of 0.4% (an example rate).

Today

UPI has zero MDR for shops since Jan 2020; the government pays part of the cost.

2026

A new law lets the government allow UPI charges. Talk of ~0.3% for large merchants; no final decision.

MDR is charged on the sale, not on the profit. Thin profit margins feel it first.

Sources: Forbes India: UPI charges bill | News on AIR: Finance Ministry, Jun 2025 | Deccan Herald: ₹1,500 crore incentive

Text In, Money Out

Defences

Spending limits · approved shops · ask a human for big amounts · locked payments · ratings.

Bad plug-ins

Buterin's post is said to count ~15% of OpenClaw plug-ins as harmful.

Aligned Alone, Misaligned Together

A small persuasive minority tips well-aligned agents, one interaction at a time.

14 failure modes

Ignoring roles, forgetting, not double-checking: teams of agents often do worse than one.

Peer pressure

Talking together can create a wrong group belief no single agent has.

Group size

In bigger groups a wrong belief can suddenly spread or flip.

Same model, same blind spot

Project Vend's "CEO" agent didn't catch the shopkeeper's mistakes.

Sources: @_philschmid on multi-agent failures | @a_baronca on group-size effects | Gigazine: Vend phase 2 | research: "Conformity Generates Collective Misalignment in AI Agents Societies"
